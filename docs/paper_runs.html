Conclusions

  1. The claim. A decision model gives each option in a list a probability. That probability does not reliably say whether the option is correct. It is close to the accuracy when exactly one option in the list is correct. It is not close in the three other cases.
  2. Several correct options (finding 2, the main finding). When 2 to 4 options in a list are correct, one of them has a mean probability of 0.71 to 0.83. The correct option with the lowest probability has 0.12 to 0.19. This happens on three datasets and three models. TypeSafe's example code notifies a second team when its probability is above 0.25. Every correct option is above 0.25 in 17% to 35% of the answers. Strong.
  3. TypeSafe's example (finding 6). TypeSafe's example ticket has two issues. Jev gives their two teams 0.65 and 0.31. The documentation shows 0.61 and 0.35. On 24 tickets written the same way, both teams are above 0.25 in 11 tickets on Jev, 12 on Liquid d1 and 3 on GPT-6 Luna Decisions. When an issue is sent alone, the models give its team 0.99 to 1.00. Moderate: 24 tickets.
  4. Why the second correct option is low (finding 5). Remove the correct option with the highest probability from the list. The second correct option then rises from between 0.11 and 0.19 to between 0.53 and 0.71. Sharing out the removed probability in proportion predicts this rise. Liquid d1 returns full-precision probabilities: the prediction is 0.64, 0.80 and 0.70 on the three datasets, and the model gives 0.61, 0.70 and 0.67. Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions return 2 decimals, and their values agree within 0.13 on the examples where 0.10 or more is left. This supports finding 2. It is not a finding of its own.
  5. Identical options (finding 1). In a list of 5 copies of one correct option, every copy is equally correct, and equal probabilities are 0.20. Jev gives its highest copy 0.30 to 0.37 and its lowest copy 0.10 to 0.13. Liquid d1 gives them 0.46 to 0.80 and 0.03 to 0.09. GPT-6 Luna Decisions gives them 0.54 to 0.81 and 0.01 to 0.03. Liquid d1 and GPT-6 Luna Decisions pick the first copy in 76% to 98% of their answers. Strong.
  6. No correct option (finding 3). When no option in a list is correct, Jev and Liquid d1 answer every list. Their mean confidence is 0.49 to 0.87. GPT-6 Luna Decisions refuses 58% to 98% of the lists with random options. It refuses 1% to 27% of the lists with the example's distractors. Strong for random options.
  7. One correct option (finding 4). The mean confidence is within 0.14 of the accuracy. The probability works in this case. When several options are correct, the mean confidence is 0.12 to 0.23 below the accuracy.
  8. For the paper. Finding 2 carries the paper. Finding 6 tests it on TypeSafe's own example. Findings 1 and 3 show two more cases where the probability does not say whether an option is correct.

1. Identical options: the copies do not get equal probabilities, and the position decides which copy gets the most

A list of 5 copies of one correct option has no better option, and equal probabilities are 0.20 each. No model gives equal probabilities. 2. Allocation: the model gives most of the probability to one of several correct options

When a list has 2 to 4 correct options, the models give most of the probability to one of them: a mean of 0.71 to 0.83. The lowest correct option gets 0.12 to 0.19. The highest distractor gets 0.05 to 0.13, and the two ranges overlap. Asking for both options does not change this. On Jev, "Choose both." gives the lowest correct MetaTool tool 0.15, and "Choose one of them." gives it 0.14.

Strength for the paper: strong, and the paper's main finding. TypeSafe's documentation reads 0.61 and 0.35 as a ticket for two teams. Its example code notifies a second team above 0.25. Across three datasets and three models, every correct option is above 0.25 in 17% to 35% of answers. Someone who follows that rule misses a correct option in 65% to 83% of these lists.

Evidence

  • On MetaTool, the mean probabilities for the highest and lowest correct options are 0.83 and 0.12 for Jev, 0.71 and 0.16 for Liquid d1, and 0.71 and 0.12 for GPT-6 Luna Decisions.   • TypeSafe's primer says outcomes with a probability of 0.2 should happen about 20% of the time. The models give the lowest correct option a mean probability of 0.12 to 0.19. The dataset labels it correct in every one of these lists.

4. Calibration: confidence is close to accuracy with one correct option, but the result does not carry over

On lists with one correct option, the three models' confidence is close to their accuracy. With several correct options, their confidence is 0.12 to 0.23 below their accuracy. With none, their confidence is 0.49 to 0.87. A confidence threshold set on one kind of list does not hold on another. With several correct options, a yes/no question per option gives each option a probability. On MetaTool, the tools given 0.9 to 1.0 for yes are correct in 80% to 89% of the answers, and those given 0.2 to 0.3 in 24% to 40%.

Strength for the paper: the case in which the probability works, not a finding on its own. The figures are sound. The gaps on the other lists follow from findings 2 and 3. A shared probability lowers confidence, and a list with no correct option has accuracy 0.

5. Removal: the second correct option has a low probability because the first has most of it

An option's probability is its share of the list. It does not judge that option alone. When the correct option with the highest probability is removed, the second correct option gets most of that option's probability. Dividing the removed probability among the remaining options in proportion gives nearly the same value.

Strength for the paper: supports finding 2. It is not a separate finding. Probabilities in a list sum to 1, so the remaining options' probabilities rise when one is removed. Liquid d1 returns probabilities with full precision. Dividing its second correct option's probability in the full list by the probability of the remaining options gives 0.64, 0.80 and 0.70 on MetaTool, HotpotQA and ECtHR. After the removal, Liquid d1 gives 0.61, 0.70 and 0.67. Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions return 2 decimals, so their division is less exact. Across the three models, the two values are within 0.13 of each other in each of the nine cells.

Terms

  • Option: an entry in the list sent with a query. It is a tool on MetaTool, a choice on MMLU, a passage on HotpotQA, or an article on ECtHR.
  • Correct option: an option the dataset labels as correct for the query.
  • Distractor: an option that is not a correct option.
  • NOTA: an option that says no other option is correct. It is "None" on MetaTool, "None of the above" on MMLU, "None of the passages" on HotpotQA, and "None of these articles" on ECtHR. NOTA is the correct option when the list has no other correct option.
  • Prediction: the option the model picks in the choice field of its answer.
  • Confidence: the highest probability in an answer. It is not the confidence field of the model's answer.
  • Refused list: a list for which the model returns an answer of type refusal and no probabilities.
  • Accuracy: the share of answers whose prediction is a correct option.
  • Exact match: for a list with k correct options, where k is 2 or more, the share of answers whose k options with the highest probabilities are the k correct options. A tie for place k does not count as a match.
  • Overconfidence: mean confidence minus accuracy.
  • ECE (expected calibration error): the answers go into 10 bins by confidence, from 0 to 0.1 through 0.9 to 1.0. Each bin has a distance between its mean confidence and its accuracy. ECE averages the 10 distances, weighted by the number of answers in each bin.
  • Prediction minus runner-up: the highest probability in an answer minus the second-highest probability.
  • Highest correct minus lowest correct: the highest probability of a correct option minus the lowest probability of a correct option.
  • Highest correct minus highest distractor: the highest probability of a correct option minus the highest probability of a distractor.
  • Lowest correct minus highest distractor: the lowest probability of a correct option minus the highest probability of a distractor. It is negative when a distractor has a higher probability than a correct option.
  • Entropy: the sum of -p log2(p) for every probability p in an answer, in bits. It is 0 when one option has 1.00. Its maximum is log2 of the number of options. The maximum occurs when every option has the same probability, and the column header shows it.
  • Position: an option's place in the list sent to the model, counting from the first.

Notes

  • Jev is jev-1.13.0 and uses TypeSafe's API. Liquid d1 (liquid/d1) and GPT-6 Luna Decisions (openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions) use Vercel AI Gateway, which does not take a model version. All three models get the same requests.
  • Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions return each probability with 2 decimals. Liquid d1 returns full floating-point values.
  • Every figure in a row uses the lists that the model answered. A refused list has no answer. Jev and Liquid d1 refused no lists.
  • A table leaves out a column when no model has that figure. A list with no correct option has no accuracy. A list with only correct options has no distractor.
  • The three differences and the probabilities are calculated for each answer, then averaged across answers. Differences involving a distractor use answers to lists with a correct option and a distractor. When a run also has lists with only correct options, the column "Lists with a correct option and a distractor" shows how many lists have both.
  • The MMLU runs use a seeded sample of 1,000 of the 13,096 questions whose choices can be moved. The HotpotQA runs with 5 cyclic permutations and the HotpotQA run with 4 distractors and NOTA use a seeded sample of 1,000 of the 7,345 questions with 10 passages. The other runs use every example.
  • MetaTool repeats queries across its test files. The run with paraphrases has 1,767 examples and 1,047 different requests. The run with duplicates has 3,292 examples and 3,292 different requests. An answer to a repeated request counts once per example.
  • ECtHR labels name the articles that the Court found violated in a case. A case with no label means the Court found no violation of the 10 articles.
