We sent the same question and passages with three Choice instructions: ``Choose every passage in the list that supports the answer to the question'' (\emph{Supports}), ``\ldots{} that is needed to answer the question'' (\emph{Needed}) and ``\ldots{} that has information that answers part of the question'' (\emph{Part of the question}). The matching yes/no questions were ``Does this passage support the answer to the question?'', ``Is this passage needed to answer the question?'' and ``Does this passage have information that answers part of the question?'', each followed by ``Passage:'' and the title and text of one passage. We sent each request three times.

Under every instruction, each model gives Rubin's passage 0.77 or more and Sharapova's 0.23 or less. Sharapova's passage gets a probability of yes of 0.00 to 0.14 under Supports and 0.52 to 0.96 under Part of the question. The three instructions differ in meaning, and the comparison does not separate wording from meaning.

Table~\ref{tab:example-invented-passages} gives the same list with invented names, tournaments, dates and locations, sent with the Supports instruction and a question about the older and about the younger player. John is older than Jane. Under the older question, Jev and Liquid d1 favor John's passage and GPT-6 Luna Decisions favors Jane's. Under the younger question, all three favor Jane's (Table~\ref{tab:example-invented}).
