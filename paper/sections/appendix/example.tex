\paragraph{Alternative instructions.}
The same question and 10 passages in Table~\ref{tab:example-passages} were sent with each Choice instruction below. Each corresponding yes/no question was asked separately for each passage and followed by ``Passage:'', its title, and its text.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Supports.} Choice: ``Choose every passage in the list that supports the answer to the question.'' Yes/no: ``Does this passage support the answer to the question?''
\item \emph{Needed.} Choice: ``Choose every passage in the list that is needed to answer the question.'' Yes/no: ``Is this passage needed to answer the question?''
\item \emph{Part of the question.} Choice: ``Choose every passage in the list that has information that answers part of the question.'' Yes/no: ``Does this passage have information that answers part of the question?''
\end{itemize}

The Choice probabilities favor Rubin's passage under every instruction and model, although Sharapova's passage is also needed to answer the question. The highest-ranked choice alone therefore misses a needed passage. For Sharapova's passage, the probability of yes ranges from 0.00 to 0.14 under Supports and from 0.52 to 0.96 under Part of the question. These instructions differ in meaning, so the comparison does not isolate a wording effect.
