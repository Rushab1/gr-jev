\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
We study Jev (\texttt{jev-1.13.0}) through TypeSafe's API, and Liquid d1 (\texttt{liquid/d1}) and GPT-6 Luna Decisions (\texttt{openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions}) through Vercel AI Gateway~\citep{vercel2026models}, which takes no model version. A request holds a query, an instruction and a list of named choices, each with a description. The answer holds the selected choice, which we call the prediction, and one probability per choice. We call the highest probability of an answer its confidence. This is not the \texttt{confidence} field of Jev's answer, which TypeSafe computes from the highest probability and the number of choices~\citep{typesafe2026confidence}. The three models received the same 38,718 requests with 207,058 questions, on 11 and 14 October 2026, and each request was sent once. Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions return probabilities with two decimals, and Liquid d1 with full precision. GPT-6 Luna Decisions can refuse a question and return no probabilities. Jev and Liquid d1 refused none.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
MetaTool~\citep{huang2024metatool} gives a query and a list of tools, with one or two correct tools. MMLU~\citep{hendrycks2021mmlu} gives a question and four choices, one of them correct; we use a seeded sample of 1,000 questions. HotpotQA in the distractor setting~\citep{yang2018hotpotqa} gives a question and up to ten passages, two of which support the answer; we use the 7,405 validation questions. ECtHR, Task~A of LexGLUE~\citep{chalkidis2022lexglue}, gives the facts of a case and ten articles of the Convention. Of its 1,000 test cases, 655 have one violated article, 192 have two to four, and 153 have none. When several choices are correct, the instruction asks for all of them, for example ``Choose every passage in the list that supports the answer to the question.'' Appendix~\ref{app:lists} gives every instruction.

\paragraph{Identical choices.}
Our main test replaces the list of an example with five copies of one of its correct choices. The query and the instruction stay the same. The copies have the same description, and their names differ only by extra spaces, which the APIs need to tell them apart. Every copy is correct and no copy is better than another, so equal probabilities are 0.20 each. Each list is sent in its five cyclic permutations, so that every copy appears once at every position. We build these lists for 3,292 MetaTool queries, the 1,000 MMLU questions, 1,000 HotpotQA questions and the 847 ECtHR cases with a violated article, which gives 30,695 lists per model. We report the mean probability of the highest and the lowest copy, the gap between them, and the share of predictions at each position. With equal probabilities the gap is 0, and each position gets 20\% of the predictions.

\paragraph{The gap between correct choices.}
We then use the lists as the datasets give them: the 497 MetaTool queries with two correct tools, and the HotpotQA questions, in their released order, and the 192 ECtHR cases with two to four violated articles, each sent in the ten cyclic permutations of its list. For each answer we take the highest and the lowest probability given to a correct choice, and report their mean difference as the gap. A uniform distribution over $n$ choices gives each choice $1/n$ and expresses no preference among them. We report the share of lists in which at least one correct choice gets less than $1/n$. This count uses the ECtHR lists in their released order only, and leaves out the 21 HotpotQA questions whose list has no distractor.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
A model is calibrated when, among the answers it gives a probability $p$, a share $p$ is correct. With one correct choice in a list we compare confidence with accuracy, the share of answers whose prediction is correct, and report the expected calibration error (ECE) over ten bins of equal width~\citep{guo2017calibration}. These lists are 16,770 MetaTool lists with one correct tool, the 4,000 MMLU lists and the 6,550 ECtHR lists of cases with one violated article. With several correct choices, calibration of the list is not defined: the probabilities sum to 1, so two correct choices cannot both get a probability close to 1. We therefore ask one yes/no question per choice, such as ``Does this passage support the answer to the question?'', and send the questions of an example together in one request. Each choice gets its own probability of yes. The questions cover the 497 MetaTool queries with two correct tools, a seeded sample of 500 HotpotQA questions and the 192 ECtHR cases with two to four violated articles. We put the answers into five bins of the probability of yes, with edges 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.7 and 1, so that every bin holds at least 50 answers. In each bin we compare the mean probability of yes with the share of answers about a correct choice. Refused questions are left out.
