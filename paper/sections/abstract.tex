Decision models return normalized probability distributions over candidate choices, which downstream applications can use to select among alternatives. Although their providers emphasize the reliability of these probabilities, calibration on tasks with a single correct choice does not establish their reliability when multiple choices are correct. We evaluate three decision models (Jev, Liquid d1, and GPT-6 Luna Decisions) on three diverse datasets (MetaTool, HotpotQA, and ECtHR) to study probability allocation and calibration. Even when models rank every correct choice above every distractor, 28\% to 50\% of those lists assign at least one correct choice a probability below the uniform baseline of \(1/n\), where \(n\) is the number of choices. We also present each model with five identical copies of a correct choice and observe substantial probability disparities, with mean gaps ranging from 17 to 79 percentage points. We further show how these allocation disparities affect the recovery of multiple correct choices under probability thresholding. These findings highlight the limitations of using decision-model probabilities to identify multiple valid alternatives, even when the models correctly distinguish them from distractors.
