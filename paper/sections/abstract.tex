Decision models return probability distributions over supplied choices, which downstream applications can use to identify multiple valid alternatives through probability thresholds. TypeSafe and Liquid AI describe their probabilities as calibrated, while OpenAI recommends using probability thresholds for downstream decisions. However, these probabilities may assign disproportionately low values to valid alternatives, causing them to be excluded by thresholding. We investigate this issue in three decision models (Jev, Liquid d1, and GPT-6 Luna Decisions) using four datasets spanning tool selection (MetaTool), multiple-choice question answering (MMLU), supporting-snippet selection (HotpotQA), and legal judgment prediction (ECtHR). We manipulate choice lists by introducing multiple correct alternatives, duplicating identical correct choices, and varying their order. When five identical correct choices are presented, the gap between the highest and lowest assigned probabilities ranges from 17 to 78 percentage points, despite their semantic equivalence. When multiple distinct correct choices are present, models concentrate probability mass on a single correct choice, with mean gaps of 55 to 74 percentage points between correct alternatives. **Our findings show that decision models advertised as producing calibrated probabilities can assign disproportionately low probabilities to valid alternatives, potentially making their distributions unreliable for identifying multiple correct choices through thresholding.**
