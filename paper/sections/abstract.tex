Decision models return normalized probability distributions over candidate choices, which downstream systems can use to select valid choices. Model providers emphasize the reliability and calibration of these probabilities, and TypeSafe AI explicitly recommends probability thresholding to identify multiple valid choices. While calibration on single-answer tasks has been independently studied, the reliability of these probabilities when multiple choices are correct remains underexplored. We evaluate three decision models—Jev, Liquid d1, and GPT-6 Luna Decisions—on three datasets: MetaTool, HotpotQA, and ECtHR, each containing lists of 10 candidate choices. Across all nine model–dataset pairs, probability thresholds select at least one correct choice in at least 98\% of lists, but achieve exact-set accuracy of only 39.2–65.0\%. Across lists with multiple valid choices, the second-highest-ranked valid choice receives 55–72 percentage points less probability than the highest-ranked valid choice. To investigate asymmetric probability allocation, we present each model with five identical copies of a correct choice and observe substantial disparities, with mean maximum–minimum probability gaps ranging from 17 to 79 percentage points. To examine whether excessive probability concentration extends to mutually exclusive choices, we evaluate the models on ChaosNLI, which provides empirical annotator vote distributions as a probabilistic reference. Even in this setting, decision models exhibit overconfidence, concentrating probability mass on a single label despite substantial human disagreement. When two labels have annotator vote shares less than 10 percentage points apart, the corresponding model probabilities differ by an average of 66 percentage points. Our findings reveal asymmetric probability allocation across correct choices, which undermines the reliable recovery of all valid choices through probability thresholding.
