\section{Conclusion}

When a list has several correct choices, decision models concentrate probability on one of them, even when every correct choice ranks above every distractor. Identical copies of a correct choice receive different probabilities and show model- and dataset-dependent position bias. Even with thresholds optimized on the evaluation data, exact set accuracy remains below the share of examples in which the models rank every correct choice above every distractor. Asking separate yes/no questions for each choice does not consistently improve this accuracy, and the resulting intermediate probabilities are miscalibrated. Choice probabilities can rank alternatives, but they should not be interpreted as the probability that each alternative is correct.
